What Would It Look Like If We All Took Off Our Masks?

CATEGORY: PERSONAL REFLECTION PUBLISHED: OCT 02, 2026

What would it look like if we all took off our masks?

“The good news means that I can stop lying to myself… Because of this, I don’t need to apply spiritual cosmetics to make myself presentable to him.”1

Everyone, whether they know it or not, wears spiritual cosmetics, some kind of mask that distorts the reality of who they are. Oftentimes, people don’t even realize they’re wearing a mask because our masks have gotten so good, we believe them ourselves. Personally, I’ve been wrestling with finding a beautiful in-between when it comes to how I see myself because here are the two extremes that each pull us towards themselves: self-loathing and pride.

The current of culture pushes towards prideful masks. Masks like, “fake it until you make it,” workaholism, and certainty. The current of church pushes the subliminal message to never speak of your strengths and to always focus on your weakness and brokenness. Boasting comes from pride, so never speak well of yourself, but rather speak negatively about yourself.

One of the most annoying parts of the Christian faith is that scripture often holds two truths in paradox. It’s always a tension. There’s an elusive, beautiful in-between that I’m looking for in my journey to take off my mask. I believe that beautiful in-between, between pride and self-loathing, is reality.

Lately, I’ve been struggling with trying to find this reality. Not thinking of myself too highly or having a "woe is me" mindset, but accepting my limitations and my strengths. No inflated ego, no insecurity. Knowing that I’m an image bearer and a fallen man. Simply holding reality. This has proved harder than I could have imagined because I’m finding that I believe the masks that I’ve created. I’ve become so good at fabricating a version of myself to show off to others that I’ve begun to believe it for myself.

I don’t know if you can relate to this struggle, but if you can, come on the journey with me. There’s no prescribed process; I don’t think there’s a formula that can deconstruct our masks. All I know is the first step: to recognize that the narratives that I believe about myself and project to the world are sometimes self-constructed defense mechanisms designed to keep me safe from rejection, pain, and shame.

Brennan Manning's book The Ragamuffin Gospel is helping me to reframe my perspective. Because the Gospel of Jesus means I don’t have to lie to myself anymore. I don’t have to prove myself, I don’t have to create false selves to protect me from rejection, pain, and shame because the cross of Jesus gives me hope. Hope that there’s a God in heaven who will never reject me. Hope that I don’t go through pain alone, but I have a friend beside me in the pain giving me purpose and endurance. Hope that despite the most detestable parts of me, the ugliest of my sin, shame no longer holds any authority.
